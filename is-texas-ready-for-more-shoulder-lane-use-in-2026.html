Frequently Asked Questions

1. Is it legal to drive on the shoulder in Texas?

Only for the specific uses the Transportation Code lists, and only when it can be done safely. Those include stopping a disabled vehicle, decelerating to turn right, accelerating to merge, letting a faster vehicle pass, passing a vehicle turning left, and following a police officer’s direction. Using the shoulder to get around slow traffic is not one of them.

 

2. Can you pass on the shoulder in Texas?

Passing on the shoulder is allowed only in the narrow case of going around a vehicle that is turning left, and only when the shoulder is clear and the move is safe. Passing a line of stopped or slow traffic on the right-hand shoulder is a citable offense and heavily damages a later injury claim.

 

3. Who is at fault in an accident on the shoulder of a Texas highway?

Fault depends on whether each driver’s presence on the shoulder was lawful and reasonable. A driver who entered the shoulder for an unpermitted reason usually carries most of it. A driver who stopped there legally can still be assigned a share if the vehicle sat too far into the lane, had no hazard lights, or stopped where it could not be seen in time.

 

4. Is it illegal to drive on the shoulder to let someone pass?

No. Moving onto the improved shoulder so an overtaking vehicle can pass is one of the permitted uses, and it is common practice on two-lane Texas highways. It still has to be done safely, which means checking that the shoulder ahead is clear of debris, disabled vehicles and pedestrians.

 

5. What should you do if you are hit while parked on the shoulder?

Stay inside the vehicle with your seatbelt on if traffic is moving close by, call 911, and let the responding officer document the position of both vehicles. Photograph the scene before anything is moved, note whether your hazard lights were running, and get checked by a doctor the same day. Report the crash to your insurer factually without accepting any share of blame.
